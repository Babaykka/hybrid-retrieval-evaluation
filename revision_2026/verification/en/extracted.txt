1 Introduction

whichdocumentssatisfyeachquery.MSMARCOisawidelyusedcollectionbuilt
from real Bing search queries [1]. Its passage-ranking task provides query and
passage texts and sparse relevance judgments, commonly called qrels, short for
query relevance judgments [2]. Most queries in the labeled development subset
have only one positive passage. Other acceptable answers may remain unjudged
and therefore contribute no gain to the evaluation metric.

2 Related Work
MSMARCOsupportsbothtrainingandevaluationofneuralretrievalmodels[1].
The mMARCO dataset extends passage ranking to multiple languages through
translation[6];itsRussianportionalsoappearsinRusBEIR[7].Ourexperiments
use English query and passage texts. Beyond a single provider, BEIR evaluates
retrieval across 18 datasets with different tasks and domains [8]. Cross-dataset
evaluation addresses transfer across collections, but does not by itself establish
demographic representativeness.
BM25 is a lexical baseline based on term matching, term frequency, and
document-length normalization [9]. Dense bi-encoders such as E5 and BGE rep-
resent a query and a document separately and compare their vectors [10,11].
Cross-encoders instead process the query and candidate jointly, allowing token-
level interactions at a higher computational cost [12]. These distinctions matter
because different systems can contribute different relevant passages to a pool.

3 Data and Manual Re-annotation
3.1 Source Collection and Candidate Pool
The English MS MARCO passage corpus contains 8,841,823 passages. The of-
ficial development qrels used here contain 7,437 positive pairs for 6,980 queries
[2]. The manual subset contains 1179 queries and 33667 distinct query–passage
pairs, with 28.56 candidates per query on average and a maximum of 63. The
larger pseudo-labeling experiment uses 101093 queries with saved ranked lists;
this set is not identical to the 6,980-query labeled development subset.
The manual pool was assembled by combining query-based retrieval results
with auxiliary candidate-selection channels. Each query has ten entries in the
saved BM25, E5, and BGE rankings. The annotation data also retain the auxil-
iary channels named e5-rerank and s0–s3. They contribute candidates beyond
the three query-only lists. In particular, 6,258 pairs have no score in BM25,
E5, BGE, or e5-rerank; 35 pools contain more than 40 documents. Thus, the
annotation pool includes additional candidates beyond the four named top-ten
lists.
Candidate preparation also used E5-based similarity to a known relevant
answer, as part of the reference-assisted annotation procedure. This signal has
access to answer information unavailable to ordinary query-only retrieval. The
full pool is retained for annotation analysis. The controlled ranking comparison
uses BM25, E5, BGE, and their three-way RRF; auxiliary channels contribute
to pooling but are excluded from system-level comparisons because their model
provenanceisnotestablished.Thefull-setrankingsuseintfloat/e5-small-v2
and BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5. These identifiers describe the full-set runs; the
exact configurations of the auxiliary manual-pool channels remain unspecified.
3.2 What the Assessor Judged
For each selected pair, the assessor saw the query, the candidate passage, and
the original relevant MS MARCO passage. The task was to decide whether the
candidate answered the same information need. Showing the reference passage
helped align the interpretation with the initial annotation; it did not require
the candidate to reproduce the reference wording. Pooling followed by these
additional judgments is the deep re-annotation studied here.

itive documents. For the sparse baseline, we use the official development qrels

4 Judge and Evaluation Protocol
4.1 Purpose and Input of the Judge
Exhaustive human assessment of tens of millions of selected pairs is impractical.
The judge transfers the signal of the manual judgments to larger pools. BM25,
E5, and BGE continue to generate the candidates; the judge assigns relevance
scorestoalreadyselectedpairs.Itsagreementwithhumanlabelsmusttherefore
be established separately from retrieval effectiveness.
We use the compact cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2 model
[16,17].1 It processes the query and candidate passage jointly, unlike the
separate encodings used by E5 and BGE. Its input contains the two texts
only: no retrieval embeddings, ranks, fusion scores, or known relevant answer
are supplied. MiniLM was selected as a public, computationally feasible MS
MARCO baseline, not as the strongest available relevance judge.

The ideal denominator is shared by all systems for that query and label set. It
is computed from all positive judgments in the pool, not only from positives
retrieved by the particular system. A query with no retrieved positives receives
zero. If the pool has no positive judgments, its NDCG is defined as zero for
every system. All reported system means use the same query set; no system-
specific successful-query filter is applied. Unjudged passages retain their rank
positions and contribute zero gain. These remain pool-based evaluations rather
than full-corpus leaderboard scores.
Manual before/after comparisons use all 1,179 queries. We calculate paired
query-level differences, 95% percentile bootstrap intervals with 5,000 resamples,
andtwo-sidedpairedsign-fliptestswith10,000randomizations.TheMonteCarlo
estimate uses the correction (b+1)/(B+1), where b is the number of random-
ized absolute effects at least as large as the observed effect. The four manual
annotation-effect tests are also checked against a Bonferroni familywise level of
0.05. Full-set intervals use the resampling settings reported with Table 5; the
query, rather than the individual passage, remains the resampling unit.

7 Limitations and Discussion
Annotation and pooling. Human assessment covers selected candidates, not the
whole corpus. The use of a known answer during candidate preparation and hu-
man judging enriches the pool and can anchor the interpretation of relevance.
This helps find alternative answers but may underrepresent other valid inter-
pretations of an ambiguous query. Exact attribution of every auxiliary channel
andindependentinter-assessoragreementareoutsidethecontrolledcomparison.
Nonstandard labels and empty texts are documented, and the query-exclusion
sensitivity check does not remove all possible annotation errors.
Judge quality. MiniLM is a baseline cross-encoder. Its held-out agreement sup-
ports using it for a controlled score-based analysis, but its pseudo-labels are
not human ground truth. Diagnostics that include training queries must not be
confused with held-out estimates. The original training’s handling of unresolved
labelcategoriesisanotherlimitation.Largerormorerecentrerankers,including
BGE cross-encoder variants, may produce different labels and system compar-
isons.
The automatic judge in this experiment receives a query and candidate only.
Thisisadeliberatelyrestrictedbaseline,notarequirementthatanassessormust
have the same information as a retrieval system. A known relevant answer can
legitimately be provided to the judge without providing it to the retriever. A
future reference-conditioned judge should therefore use “query, known relevant

is a natural next step [8]; multilingual translation alone would not establish
demographic coverage.
8 Conclusion
Deep re-annotation in this study means increasing the coverage of human rele-
vance judgments inside a defined candidate pool. On 1,179 queries, additional
judgments increase the mean positive count from 1.05 to 17.47. Measured
NDCG@10 rises for BM25, E5, BGE, and RRF(3) when the same rankings and
query set are evaluated against these denser labels. E5 remains the best of the
three query-only retrievers in the manual experiment.
The MiniLM judge enables a larger controlled pseudo-labeling analysis and
reaches held-out ROC-AUC 0.907. Its input is intentionally restricted to query
and candidate text. Future work should compare stronger cross-encoders and
a judge conditioned on the known relevant answer, using the format “query,
known relevant answer, candidate passage”. Such a study would test the value
of reference information for assessment rather than assume that the current
baseline is optimal.
Candidate-pooldepthisessentialforinterpretingthefull-setresults.RRF(3)
is below E5 at input depth ten but above it at the four larger tested depths
under the judge’s labels. Positive-label counts depend on which documents are
scored, and rank fusion also depends on the depth of its input lists. Neither a
highpseudo-positivecountnorafusionadvantagecanbetransferredtoanother
evaluation setting without checking these conditions.
In practical terms, additional annotation and automatic pseudo-labeling are
useful research instruments when the candidate selection, normalization, thresh-
old,andevaluationpopulationareexplicit.Re-evaluatingMSMARCOtherefore
concerns both improving judgment coverage and defining a consistent measure-
ment procedure, with transfer beyond this collection left to further empirical
validation.
