## Defensible Contribution

This is an empirical study of evaluation sensitivity: answer-assisted manual re-annotation of a defined MS MARCO pool, followed by a controlled saved-score pseudo-label analysis that distinguishes assessment coverage from fusion input truncation. It does not introduce pooling, RRF, cross-encoders, a new retrieval architecture, or a demonstrated state-of-the-art judge. The supplied evidence supports no "first-ever" claim. A reference-conditioned automatic judge remains a proposed experiment, not a completed contribution. [Corrected research questions](./delivery_revision/manuscript_en.tex:37).

**Deep re-annotation:** assessing multiple selected candidate answers per query, including alternatives absent from sparse qrels. "Deep" describes coverage within the pool, not network depth, more queries, exhaustive corpus annotation, or proven error-free labels.

**Pooling versus retrieval:** retrieval produces ordered candidates; pooling merges candidates for assessment; RRF constructs another ranking; the judge scores selected pairs. The manual answer-assisted pool and the full-set BM25/E5/BGE union are different constructions. Auxiliary channels can contribute to annotation without becoming valid comparable retrieval systems.

**Information access:** human assessors see query, candidate and known relevant answer; candidate preparation also uses answer information. The implemented judge receives query and candidate only, with no reference, retrieval scores or ranks. Its supervision and candidate distribution still inherit the answer-assisted procedure. Providing a reference to an assessor is legitimate and need not imply giving that reference to the retriever; the missing experiment is whether a reference-conditioned judge improves held-out agreement. [Input specification](./delivery_revision/manuscript_en.tex:104), [future judge](./delivery_revision/manuscript_en.tex:219).

These intervals describe paired changes across queries, not uncertainty bars for either separate mean or variation across training runs. E5 has the highest measured human-label NDCG among the three compared query-only retrievers. [Manual metrics](./revision_2026/results/manual_summary.json:231).

- Human positives: 1,243 to 20,597; means 1.05 to 17.47 over the same 1,179 queries and 33,667 selected pairs. This measures pool coverage, not corpus relevance density.
- At K=200: 45,647,587 scored candidate pairs; 13,004,336 pseudo-positives; mean 128.64 across all 101,093 queries. The conditional mean 130.70 excludes 1,592 zero-positive queries and must not be substituted without changing the label. Use "pseudo-positive passages per query," never unqualified "relevant documents per query." [Full-set endpoint](./revision_2026/results/fullset_summary.json:1958).
- Unjudged passages retain their original rank and receive zero gain; zero-positive queries remain in system means. These are pool-based metrics, not directly comparable leaderboard scores.
- The RRF advantage is positive at the four tested depths 30, 50, 100 and 200; it is not a monotonic improvement with depth. E5's top-ten DCG remains 3.7130 while its NDCG decreases as the common ideal denominator grows. More pseudo-positives alone do not establish higher annotation accuracy.
- No exhaustive relevance, independent multi-assessor consensus, stronger/reference-conditioned judge result, cross-provider transfer, geographic/social representativeness or universal fusion superiority is established.
