1 Introduction
Modern neural information retrieval systems require large collections of queries,
documents, and relevance judgments. One of the most influential collections of
this kind is MS MARCO, built from real Bing search queries and widely used
for passage and document ranking [1]. The mMARCO dataset, its multilingual
extension, includes Russian [2], and the Russian portion is used in RusBEIR, a
benchmark for Russian information retrieval [3].
MSMARCOisoneofseveralsearch-providerdatasets,notauniversalsample
of information needs. Google’s Natural Questions contains real search queries
with human answer annotations on Wikipedia pages [4]. Yahoo!’s Learning to
Rank Challenge released two sets of query–document feature vectors and graded
relevance labels without query or document text [5]. Yandex’s Personalized Web
Search Challenge released session and click logs with anonymized identifiers

search research, but their tasks and released representations differ from passage

WefocusonMSMARCObecauseitsaccessibletexts,sparsehumanjudgments,
established evaluation tools, and pretrained models support comparisons with
prior work. The sparse judgments also provide a direct setting for studying
additional annotation. This choice does not establish universality: provider-
specific sampling, language, query intent, and document domain may affect
transfer. Differences between countries or social groups are not measured in this
study.
A central property of MS MARCO is the sparsity of its original relevance
judgments. In the passage ranking task, most queries are associated with a
single positive passage that may answer the user’s question [7]. The standard file
containing query-document relevance labels is commonly called qrels, short for
query relevance judgments. This design is practical for large-scale training, but it
can be incomplete for evaluation: other relevant passages may exist in the corpus
but remain unjudged. A retrieval model may therefore return a useful document
and still receive no credit because the document is absent from the qrels file.
This incompleteness matters especially when comparing systems of different
types. Lexical methods such as BM25 rely on term matching [8]; dense retrievers
suchasE5andBGEuseseparatevectorrepresentationsofqueriesanddocuments
[9,10];andcross-encodersscorequery-documentpairsjointly[11].Thesemethods
may retrieve different relevant documents. Re-evaluating MS MARCO after
additional annotation is therefore not merely a matter of increasing absolute
scores; it can also change the comparative picture between retrieval approaches.
By deep re-annotation we mean extending relevance judgments across the
candidate documents in a defined pool. “Deep” refers to annotation coverage,
not neural-network depth or repeated training. It does not mean that the entire
corpus has been exhaustively judged. For example, a query about why a liquid’s
boiling point increases may have several passages explaining the addition of salt.
If only one passage is in the original qrels, another correct explanation receives
zero gain until it is judged. Section 3.3 gives an actual example with passage
identifiers and before/after labels.
The retrieval rankings are held fixed when comparing original and human
labels.Thus,anincreaseinmeasuredNDCGafterannotationdoesnotmeanthat
aretrievalmodelitselfhasimproved.Itmeansthatitsexistingoutputisevaluated
against more complete judgments. A second part of the study uses a learned
judge to extend label coverage to larger candidate pools. These predictions are
called pseudo-labels to distinguish them from human judgments.
We study this problem at two levels. First, we analyze a manual subset for
whichadditionalhumanrelevancejudgmentsareavailable.Thismakesitpossible
to compare the original MS MARCO labels with denser human labels inside the
same candidate pool. Second, we study a larger query set using a judge model:
a binary classifier that receives a query-document pair and returns a relevance
score. This judge is used because manually annotating the full set is impractical.

We also analyze Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) [12], a simple method for
combiningrankedlists.RRFassignsahigherscoretodocumentsthatappearnear
the top of several input rankings. In this paper, RRF is used to test whether a

The study addresses three research questions.
1. How does additional manual annotation change qrels density and retrieval
quality metrics?
2. How reliable is a judge model as a tool for automatic pseudo-labeling?
3. How does pooling depth affect the number of pseudo-relevant documents and
the quality of RRF-based fusion?
By pooling depth we mean the number of top documents taken from each
ranked list before merging. For example, at K =10 the judge receives the union
of the top 10 documents from each system, while at K = 200 it receives a
much larger union. Thus, the density of pseudo-relevance labels in the full-set
experiment is a property of the pooling protocol, not only of the corpus or the
judge.
The main findings are as follows. Manual re-annotation significantly increases
NDCG@10 for all ranking sources considered in the manual pool. The MiniLM-
based cross-encoder judge [13,14] achieves sufficient held-out quality to serve as
a reproducible baseline for pseudo-labeling. On the full query set, pseudo-qrels
density is determined not only by corpus properties and judge quality, but also
by the pooling protocol. Finally, RRF is useful only on a small controlled pool;
its advantage disappears as the candidate pool expands.
2 Related Work
MS MARCO was introduced as a large-scale dataset based on real search queries
and has become a standard testbed for text ranking [1]. The mMARCO dataset
extends it through machine translation to several languages [2]. For Russian,
RusBEIR collects translated, adapted, and original Russian retrieval datasets for
benchmark evaluation [3].
BM25 remains a standard lexical retrieval baseline, ranking documents
through term frequency and document length normalization [8]. Dense retrieval
methodsrepresentqueriesanddocumentsasvectors;widelyusedfamiliesinclude
E5 [9] and BGE/FlagEmbedding [10]. Cross-encoders process a query and a
document jointly and are commonly used for re-ranking candidate lists [11].
Pooling is a standard approach for building information retrieval test collec-
tions: documents from the top ranks of several systems are merged and then
judged by assessors [15]. Pooling reduces annotation cost, but it also makes the
resulting qrels dependent on the systems and pool depth used to construct the
pool.
Rank fusion is another related line of work. RRF was proposed as a simple
way to combine ranked outputs from different retrieval systems without training

additionalparameters[12].Laterworkonhybridretrievalandretrieval-augmented
generation has shown that RRF remains practically useful, but its effect depends
on the input systems, list depth, and fusion parameters [16,17,18].
Our work does not introduce a new retrieval model. Its contribution is a
re-evaluation of existing ranking sources after additional annotation, an analysis
of automatic pseudo-labeling with a judge model, and an explicit separation
between judge quality and candidate-pool depth.
3 Data, Pooling, and Manual Re-annotation
3.1 Source Collection
The source material is MS MARCO Passage Ranking. It contains user queries, a
corpus of 8,841,823 short passages, and qrels files, i.e., query relevance judgment
files that specify documents considered relevant to the queries. The original
development-set qrels contain 7,437 positive pairs for 6,980 queries. The manual
subset contains 1,179 queries and 33,667 distinct query–passage pairs, with 28.56
candidates per query on average and a maximum of 63. The full-set candidate-
pool protocol considered in this paper is larger: it uses 101,093 queries for which
saved BM25, E5, and BGE rankings are available for pseudo-labeling.
The issue with the original labels is not that the positive documents are
necessarily wrong, but that they often do not exhaust all acceptable answers
in the corpus. If a query asks which county a place belongs to, the corpus may
contain multiple passages that correctly state the same county. The original qrels
may include only one of them. A system that retrieves another correct passage is
then penalized because that passage is unjudged.
The manual pool was assembled by combining query-based retrieval results
with auxiliary candidate-selection channels. Each query has ten entries in the
savedBM25,E5,andBGErankings.Theannotationdataalsoretaintheauxiliary
channels E5-rerank and E5-based similarity to a known relevant answer, as part
ofthereference-assistedannotationprocedure.Thesimilaritysignalhasaccessto
answer information unavailable to ordinary query-only retrieval. They contribute
candidates beyond the three query-only lists. In particular, 6,258 pairs have no
scoreinBM25,E5,BGE,orE5-rerank;35poolscontainmorethan40documents.
Thus, the annotation pool includes additional candidates beyond the four named
top-ten lists.

3.3 Dataset Characteristics

query. After manual re-annotation, the number of positive pairs increases to
20,597, or 17.47 relevant documents per query. Thus, the new evaluation set does
not replace MS MARCO; rather, it shows how strongly qrels density changes
within a deliberately constructed pool.

4.1 Judge Model
The judge model is introduced to transfer the manual annotation signal to
candidate pools that are too large to annotate exhaustively by humans. It is

not used as an additional retrieval system and does not replace BM25, E5, or
BGE. Those systems generate candidate documents; the judge assigns binary
pseudo-relevance labels to already selected query-document pairs. Therefore, the
judge is evaluated as a pairwise relevance classifier rather than as a first-stage
retriever on the original qrels.
The judge is a cross-encoder: the query and the candidate passage are pro-
cessed jointly by the transformer. This differs from E5 and BGE, which are
bi-encoder retrievers that encode queries and documents separately and compare
their vector representations. A cross-encoder is slower, but it can model token-
levelinteractionsbetweenthe queryandthecandidatedocumentand istherefore
commonly used for pairwise relevance assessment.
The judge model is based on cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2,
a compact MiniLM-based cross-encoder distributed through the Sentence-
Transformers model collection [13,14].1 We use it because it is public, repro-
ducible, already trained for MS MARCO passage ranking, and computationally
feasible for a controlled re-annotation study. The unmodified base model reaches
ROC-AUC 0.865 on our validation split; after fine-tuning on the manual labels,
validation ROC-AUC increases to 0.908. The judge receives only the text of the
query and the text of the candidate document. Embeddings, ranks, and scores
from BM25, E5, BGE, or RRF are not passed to the judge; these systems are
used only for candidate generation and subsequent comparison.
The judge was fine-tuned on the manual subset of 1,179 queries and 33,667
pairs. The splitwas performed byquery identifiers in a70/15/15 proportion with
random seed 42: 23,543 pairs from 825 queries for training, 5,122 pairs from 177
queriesforvalidation,and5,002pairsfrom177queriesfortesting.Alldocuments
of the same query were assigned to the same split. The binarization threshold
was selected on the validation part and equals τ =−0.2187.

6 Results of Manual Re-annotation
6.1 Qrels Density Shift
The first central result is that deep re-annotation substantially changes qrels
density. On the manual subset, the average number of relevant documents per
query increases from 1.07 to 17.47, and the median increases from 1 to 17.5. For
judgelabelsonthesamemanualpool,themedianincreasesfurtherto19.Onthe
full pseudo-labeled set, the median reaches 93 and the 99th percentile reaches
401.
The value of about 130 pseudo-relevant documents per query should not be
read as a direct estimate of the corpus’ true relevance density. It appears in the
full protocol at K =200, where the average pool size is 451.54 documents per
query. The observed density is therefore a joint consequence of judge behavior
and candidate presentation.
6.2 Ranking Quality Change
Manual re-annotation significantly increases NDCG@10 for all ranking sources in
the manual pool. This includes lexical retrieval, dense retrieval, the seed-answer
auxiliarysignal,andRRF.Judge-basedlabelsonthemanualpoolfurtherincrease
the metric values, but those values must be interpreted separately from human
labels because they include judge errors.
Tables 2 and 4, together with Fig. 2, show that the increase is systematic. All
listed paired permutation tests produce p<0.001, and the bootstrap confidence
intervals do not cross zero.

8 Threats to Validity and Discussion
Construct validity. Deep re-annotation changes both metric values and system
comparisons.SparseMSMARCOqrelscanunderestimatestrongdenseretrievers
and fusion methods. At the same time, both human and pseudo labels depend
on which documents were presented for judgment.
Protocol validity. High-density pseudo-qrels on the full set cannot be discussed
independently of pooling depth. The value of roughly 130 positive judgments per
query is a property of the specific full-set protocol, not a natural constant of MS
MARCO.
Judge validity. The judge analysis combines held-out classification metrics with
score diagnostics on the annotated pool. These pieces of evidence are comple-

The selected MiniLM cross-encoder should be read as a reproducible baseline
rather than as the strongest possible judge. More recent or larger rerankers,
including BGE cross-encoder variants, may produce different pseudo-qrels and
should be compared in a separate controlled experiment. Another natural exten-
sion is a relevance-answer-conditioned judge that receives the known relevant MS
MARCO passage together with the candidate document, because the manual-
pooling results show that similarity to the seed answer is a strong signal. We
do not use such a signal in the full-set protocol, since using a known relevant
document at evaluation time would give the judge external answer information
that is unavailable to an ordinary query-only retrieval system.
External validity. The full-set part is limited to BM25, E5, and BGE, because
these systems produce query-only rankings. The seed-answer signal is used only
for manual pooling and is not a deployable full-corpus retrieval system. All
conclusions are specific to MS MARCO and to the candidate selection protocol
studied here.
Practical implications. The results answer the three research questions. First,
manualre-annotationsignificantlyincreasesNDCG@10andqrelsdensity.Second,
the MiniLM judge is a usable baseline for pseudo-labeling, but its threshold
and held-out quality must be controlled. Third, the interpretation of the 130.7
figure is inseparable from pooling depth, and RRF is useful only under a small
controlled pool.
9 Conclusion
This paper shows that deep re-annotation on MS MARCO substantially changes
qrels density and significantly increases NDCG@10 for all ranking sources con-
sidered in the manual pool. On this pool, the highest score is achieved by an
auxiliary signal that uses the original relevant answer, while E5 remains the best
ordinary query-only retrieval system. The MiniLM judge provides a reproducible
baseline for automatic pseudo-labeling.
The full-set experiment deliberately uses a strict query-only setting for au-
tomatic pseudo-labeling: the judge receives a query and a candidate document,

14  et al.
but not the original relevant MS MARCO passage. This restriction keeps the
protocol comparable to ordinary retrieval outputs and avoids giving the judge
external answer information that ordinary retrieval systems do not have. At the
same time, the manual-pooling results show that the known relevant answer
is a strong signal. A natural direction for future work is therefore to study a
relevance-answer-conditioned judge with the input format “query, known relevant
answer, candidate document under assessment”, and to compare it with stronger
cross-encoder models.
For the full-set protocol, pooling depth is a key factor in interpreting the
results.Withasmallpool,RRFisthebestavailableconfiguration,butitsquality
decreases monotonically as the pool expands. Pseudo-qrels density and fusion
gains should therefore be analyzed together with the candidate selection protocol.
In practical terms, full-corpus pseudo-labeling is useful as a research instru-
ment, but it requires controlled candidate pools and explicit restrictions when
conclusions are stated. Thus, re-evaluating MS MARCO should be discussed
not only as improving relevance judgments, but also as refining the evaluation
protocol itself.
